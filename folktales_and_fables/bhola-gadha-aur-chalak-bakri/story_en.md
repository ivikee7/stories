# Story Script: The Innocent Donkey and the Clever Goat
### (भोला गधा और चालाक बकरी – दोस्ती, लालच और अक्ल की परीक्षा)

**Genre**: Dramatic Moral Folktale / Children's & Family Animated Story  
**Visual Style**: 16:9 Widescreen 3D Pixar Animation, Zero Text, Single-Shot Illustrations  
**Pacing**: Calm Storytelling Voiceover (110–130 WPM)  
**Audio Architecture**: Dual Audio Female Storytelling (Track 1: Hindi `hi-IN-SwaraNeural`, Track 2: English `en-US-JennyNeural`)  

---

## Characters
1. **Bhola (The Donkey)**: Humble, hardworking, gentle, and loyal grey donkey. Possesses deep inner wisdom and quiet courage.
2. **Chanchal (The Goat)**: Swift, spotted black-and-white goat. Sharp-minded, witty, but inherently selfish and scheming.
3. **Deendayal (The Potter)**: Kind, elderly village potter who loves his animals like family.
4. **The Greedy Wolf**: Menacing predator with glowing amber eyes, ruthless yet blinded by appetite.
5. **The Village Farmer**: Stern, protective landowner with a heavy bamboo staff.

---

## Prologue: Rampur Village at Dawn (00:00 – 03:00)
`[VISUAL: Wide panoramic sunrise over Rampur village, terracotta clay pots drying by thatch cottages, winding river reflecting golden morning light]`  
`[SFX: Gentle river ripples, morning birds chirping, distant temple bells]`

Welcome, dear listeners... On this tranquil evening, let me take you on a journey to a timeless village, where the evening breeze carries the earthy fragrance of wet soil, and where the animals think, understand, and speak with voices of their own.

This is the story of a lush and peaceful hamlet named 'Rampur'. By the village edge flowed a broad, glistening river, and beyond that river stretched a deep, mysterious forest. In this village lived a humble potter named Deendayal. Deendayal was a kind-hearted, gentle, and hardworking man. He lived with two special companions—his loyal pet donkey, named 'Bhola', and a nimble, spotted goat with black and white patches, named 'Chanchal'.

`[VISUAL: Potter Deendayal shaping wet clay on his wheel with Bhola resting nearby and Chanchal prancing in courtyard]`

Bhola the donkey was truly innocent, diligent, and faithful, just as his name suggested. From dawn to dusk, he hauled smooth wet clay from the riverbank, and later carried baked terracotta pots safely to the village marketplace. He never once uttered a complaint. Whatever fodder came his way, he ate gratefully, and rested peacefully in his sheltered corner.

On the other hand was Chanchal the goat. Chanchal was extraordinarily swift, clever, and inherently selfish. Her sharp mind was always scheming for personal gain and mischievous thrills. Throughout the day, she roamed the village alleys, nibbling on fresh tender leaves, and in the evening, she sat by Bhola, spinning tall tales of the world.

Bhola regarded her as his dearest companion. He shared every thought and worry with Chanchal. Yet, what truly lay behind Chanchal's cunning smile, honest Bhola could never foresee.

## Chapter 1: The Scorch of Drought & Unbound Freedom (03:00 – 08:00)
`[VISUAL: Cracked dry soil, blazing summer sun heat haze, parched fields with wilted grass]`  
`[SFX: Hot wind rustle, distant dry cricket trills]`

Then came the harsh days of summer. The scorching sun blazed mercilessly across the skies. Gradually, the village ponds evaporated into cracked mud, and the vibrant greenery vanished under the searing heat. The potter Deendayal's livelihood dwindled rapidly, for in such a severe drought, no villager could spare money for new clay pots.

Soon, Deendayal had no fodder left to feed his beloved animals. Anxiety weighed heavily upon his heart. One evening, gazing at his empty grain bags, Deendayal sighed to his wife, "My dear, if this drought continues, how will we feed ourselves, let alone these innocent souls? Tomorrow morning, I shall untie Bhola and Chanchal, allowing them to graze freely across the riverbanks so they may nourish themselves."

Tied in the corner, Chanchal overheard every word. Her heart leaped with excitement at the thought of boundless freedom to wander. But Bhola felt a pang of sorrow, deeply worried for his master's well-being.

`[VISUAL: Deendayal gently untying the hemp ropes from Bhola and Chanchal's collars under morning sunlight]`

The following morning, Deendayal gently untied the ropes around their necks. With a heavy heart and trembling hands, he patted them warmly, "Go forth, my dear ones. Find tender grass along the river or near the forest edge. But promise me you will return safely before dusk falls."

Together, Bhola and Chanchal stepped out onto the dusty trail. Along the path, Chanchal pranced and leaped with excitement, while Bhola walked with slow, thoughtful steps.

Chanchal looked back and laughed, "Oh, come on, Bhola! Why the long face? Look around us—today we are free! No heavy loads of wet clay, and no master's scolding!"

Bhola let out a deep breath and murmured, "Chanchal, freedom brings me no joy when our master is suffering. If we don't work, how will his household survive?"

Chanchal scoffed playfully, "You will always remain a naive donkey, Bhola! Humans always manage to look after themselves. Think of your own empty stomach! Look across the river—behold that vast, emerald green field. Come, let us go there!"

Bhola looked across the glistening river. Indeed, on the far bank lay a farmer's thriving field, laden with sweet corn and crisp green vegetables.

Bhola hesitated, alarmed, "No, Chanchal! That field belongs to a hardworking farmer. Grazing on someone's crop without permission is theft. If the farmer catches us, his wooden staff will show no mercy."

Chanchal rolled her eyes and adopted a silky, persuasive tone, "Oh, my innocent friend, the farmer is surely enjoying a deep afternoon nap right now. We shall only nibble a few sweet leaves and quietly slip away. Look at you—your ribs are showing! If you grow weak from hunger, how will you ever serve our master?"

## Chapter 2: The Cornfield Feast & The Folly of Braying (08:00 – 13:00)
`[VISUAL: Wading across glistening shallow river into towering green stalks of sweet corn]`  
`[SFX: Water splashing, rustling corn leaves, satisfying crunching sounds]`

Swayed by Chanchal's sweet and convincing words, Bhola relented. Side by side, they waded across the shallow river and entered the lush cornfield.

Within moments, both began feasting on the tender, juicy leaves. For Bhola, who had starved on dry straw for weeks, these crisp sweet leaves tasted like heavenly nectar. He ate and ate, until his hollow belly became wonderfully full and round.

Chanchal, ever cunning, ate cautiously. After every two bites, she perked up her ears and glanced around, ensuring she grazed close to the boundary fence for a swift getaway.

On the other hand, simple-minded Bhola was intoxicated with satisfaction. A feeling of sheer bliss washed over him. He looked at Chanchal and beamed, "Ah, Chanchal! My heart is brimming with immense joy today. When the stomach is full and the breeze is cool, divine music awakens within my soul. I feel an irresistible urge to sing a glorious song!"

Chanchal froze in terror. She whispered urgently, "Are you out of your mind, Bhola?! Stop this madness! You are a donkey, not a nightingale! When you sing, you don't produce sweet melodies—you bray with a deafening noise! If you make a sound, the farmer will wake up, and we will both be thrashed!"

Bhola shook his head with childish stubbornness, "Chanchal, you know nothing of high art! You are merely jealous of my vocal talents. Just listen to the power of my melody!"

Saying this, Bhola raised his long snout toward the sky, closed his eyes in pure ecstasy, and let out an ear-shattering bray: "Hee-haw! Hee-haw! Hee-haw!"

`[SFX: Ear-shattering donkey braying echo across the valley, sudden furious farmer shout]`

The loud braying echoed across the entire valley like a thunderclap. At the far end of the field, the sleeping farmer jolted upright in alarm. Grabbing his heavy bamboo staff, he rushed furiously toward the cornfield.

The moment Chanchal saw the enraged farmer storming toward them, she slipped beneath the wooden fence without warning Bhola and vanished into the thick thorny bushes.

Poor Bhola, his eyes still closed in musical delight, had no idea what was coming. Suddenly, a heavy wooden blow landed squarely upon his back!

`[SFX: Dull heavy thwacks of bamboo staff on back, donkey bray of pain]`

"Thwack! Thwack!" The farmer struck with all his fury.

Bhola cried out in agony. Terror replaced his musical dreams as he frantically scrambled to escape. Dodging blow after blow, he somehow plunged into the river and dragged his bruised body to the safety of the opposite bank. His back was covered with painful welts, and he limped heavily on his aching legs.

Hiding safely behind the bushes, Chanchal emerged with a mocking smirk, "Didn't I warn you, my dear singer? Now look at your reward for your lovely concert!"

Tears welled in Bhola's gentle eyes, but in his innocence, he blamed only himself, thinking he should have listened to his clever friend.

## Chapter 3: Flight into the Wild & The Prowling Wolf (13:00 – 17:00)
`[VISUAL: Deepening twilight shadows, dense ancient forest path, swirling evening fog]`  
`[SFX: Low eerie wind, nocturnal owl hoot, sudden menacing predator growl]`

As the sun began to sink behind the hills, long purple shadows stretched across the land. The quiet village felt far away, and the cooling air turned brisk.

Chanchal looked around and said, "Bhola, if we return home now, our master will ask where we have been, and seeing your bruises, he might scold us. Let us venture toward the dense jungle. There we will find wild berries and shelter for the night."

Limpy and exhausted, Bhola agreed, placing full trust in his friend. Together, they walked into the deep shadows of the wild forest.

The jungle grew thicker and darker with every step. Towering ancient trees blocked the fading light, and eerie nocturnal sounds whispered through the rustling leaves.

Suddenly, two glowing amber eyes appeared in the twilight mist!

It was a fierce, ravenous gray wolf! His sharp fangs glistened, and a low, guttural growl rumbled from his throat as he stalked silently toward them along the narrow trail.

Chanchal's sharp eyes spotted the predator first. A cold shiver of terror gripped her spine. The wolf stood directly on the trail leading back toward the village—there was nowhere to run!

Her cunning mind raced frantically: "If I run, the wolf will easily overtake me because I am small. But if I trap Bhola in front of him, the wolf will feast upon the large donkey, and I will escape completely unharmed!"

Chanchal quickly stepped beside Bhola and whispered in a trembling voice, "Bhola, do not look now, but a deadly wolf is blocking our path!"

Bhola's legs quaked with dread, "What?! A wolf? What shall we do, Chanchal? Let us run for our lives!"

Chanchal stopped him with false confidence, "No, Bhola! If we turn our backs and run, he will pounce instantly. Listen to my plan: I am small and harmless, so he will ignore me. But you are big, sturdy, and imposing. Stand tall, puff up your chest, and stomp toward him braying fiercely! He will think you are a terrifying jungle beast and flee in fright. Meanwhile, I will climb behind and roll heavy stones to create a deafening commotion!"

In his innocent panic, Bhola believed her. Gathering every ounce of courage, he raised his head and stepped forward toward the approaching beast.

`[VISUAL: Chanchal leaping up steep rocky cliff ledge to high safety while Bhola stands alone in clearing]`

Yet the moment Bhola stepped forward, Chanchal did not roll stones. Instead, she leaped swiftly onto a narrow mountain crevice and scrambled up a steep, towering cliff—far beyond the reach of any wolf! Perched high above, she looked down safely, watching Bhola walk straight toward his doom.

## Chapter 4: The Climax of Wit & The Thunderous Kick (17:00 – 19:30)
`[VISUAL: Wolf baring razor fangs stalking closer, Bhola calmly groaning and raising his hind leg]`  
`[SFX: Menacing wolf snarl, quiet tension drone]`

The wolf stalked closer, licking his hungry chops, and sneered, "Well, well, well... What an unusual donkey you are! Animals flee in terror when they see me, yet you limp directly into my jaws! Have you lost your mind, or are you eager to become my dinner?"

Bhola glanced back for Chanchal's promised support, only to see her sitting comfortably atop the high cliff ledge, watching silently without lifting a pebble.

In that heart-stopping moment, the veil of innocence fell from Bhola's eyes. He realized the bitter truth: Chanchal had abandoned him to be devoured so she could save her own skin!

There was no one to rescue him. If he panicked now, his life was forfeit. A sudden spark of profound wisdom and calm resolve ignited within the gentle donkey's heart.

Bhola stopped in his tracks, lowered his head with an expression of agonizing pain, and let out a piteous groan. He raised his right hind hoof off the ground, trembling as if in unbearable torment.

The wolf paused, intrigued and cautious, "Hey donkey! Why are you groaning and lifting your hoof?"

Bhola spoke with a weak, trembling, yet utterly convincing voice, "O mighty king of the forest! I know very well that my time on this earth has come to an end, and I am destined to be your royal feast today. I surrender willingly to my fate."

The wolf pricked his ears, pleased by the donkey's respectful surrender, "Then why are you making such strange faces?"

Bhola sighed deeply and said, "Mighty wolf, while fleeing through the briars, a large, poisonous iron thorn pierced deep into the sole of my hind hoof. It throbs with deadly venom. If you eat me now with this sharp thorn inside my hoof, it will puncture your throat and poison your stomach, causing you unbearable agony. Before you take your first bite, I beg of you—kindly pull out this wretched thorn with your sharp teeth so that I may die peacefully, and you may savor your meal in perfect safety!"

The greedy wolf thought for a moment. The donkey was already crippled and cornered; there was nowhere he could run. The prospect of having a venomous thorn lodge in his throat sounded genuinely dangerous.

"You speak wisely, donkey," the wolf growled. "Stand still while I extract the thorn!"

The wolf crept behind Bhola and lowered his snout right next to the raised hoof, opening his jaws wide to inspect the sole.

At that exact split second, when the wolf's nose was mere inches away, Bhola summoned every ounce of strength from his entire body. With the fury of lightning, he unleashed a thunderous, bone-shattering double-hoof kick straight into the wolf's exposed jaw!

`[SFX: Massive impact CRACK-BOOM, sickening bone crunch, howling shriek of wolf pain]`

"CRACK-BOOM!"

The ferocious kick caught the wolf squarely across his snout and teeth! The impact was so colossal that the wolf was lifted into the air and hurled backward into the thorny briars with broken fangs and a shattered jaw!

Howling in blinding agony and utter terror, the defeated wolf scrambled to his feet and bolted into the deep forest darkness, never daring to look back!

High on the cliff ledge, Chanchal stood frozen in absolute disbelief. Her eyes were wide with shock. The donkey she had always mocked as a slow, foolish beast had, through sheer patience and brilliant wit, overpowered a ruthless predator!

---

## Chapter 5: Unveiled Truth, Monsoon Reunion & Eternal Moral (19:30 – 21:09)
`[VISUAL: Chanchal climbing down with sheepish fake smile; Bhola standing tall and dignified]`  
`[SFX: Distant rolling thunder, cool fresh wind gust]`

Once the wolf had completely vanished, Chanchal slowly descended from the rocky cliff, plastering a sweet, deceitful smile across her face.

Chanchal clapped her hooves theatrically, "Oh, Bravo, my dear Bhola! What a spectacle! Didn't I tell you? My trick worked brilliantly! I climbed up to the cliff solely to scout the area and ensure no other wolves were ambushing you!"

Bhola turned slowly and looked into Chanchal's eyes. Today, the blindfold of gullibility was gone forever. He spoke in a calm, steady, and resolute voice:

"Chanchal, do not insult my intelligence any further. I saw clearly that you left me to die, using my life as a shield to secure your own escape."

Chanchal stammered in flustered embarrassment, "No, no, Bhola, you misunderstand! I was only trying to—"

Bhola gently raised his hoof, cutting her off, "Save your honeyed words, Chanchal. Today, I have received the greatest lesson of my life. A companion who abandons you at the first sign of peril, and recklessly endangers your life for their own selfish greed, is not a friend at all—they are the most dangerous of enemies. I may be a humble donkey, but today I have learned that not every tongue coated in honey wishes you well."

Chanchal burned with overwhelming shame. Her cleverness had failed her completely, and she stood speechless with her head hung low.

`[VISUAL: Torrential monsoon rain pouring over Rampur, Deendayal joyfully embracing Bhola in courtyard]`  
`[SFX: Heavy rhythmic rainfall, thunder, joyful emotional swells]`

Bhola turned his back on false friendship and walked serenely along the path back to the village. As he entered the courtyard, potter Deendayal ran forward with tears of pure joy, wrapping his arms around his faithful companion.

And as destiny would have it, that very night, dark thunderclouds rolled across the heavens, unleashing torrential monsoon rains! The prolonged drought was broken, the parched fields flourished with vibrant green life, and Deendayal's pottery wheel hummed once again with prosperity.

Bhola lived on, wiser, alert, and deeply cherished by his master. He worked diligently, lived happily, and never again fell prey to flattering deceit. And Chanchal the goat? She remained isolated and alone, having lost her truest, most faithful friend to her own selfish cunning.

And so, dear listeners, this timeless tale leaves us with an invaluable truth: The true measure of friendship is revealed only in times of adversity. Never place blind trust in those whose affection exists only when it serves their own advantage. And above all—never underestimate someone merely because they are quiet, humble, or kind; for when crisis arrives, patience, presence of mind, and inner courage can triumph over the greatest perils of this world.
